## LinkedIn Post
There's a scene in the Mahabharata that's really a leadership lesson in disguise.

Bhima — the strongest man alive, used to solving everything by force — is charging through a forest and finds an old monkey blocking his path. He orders it to move. The monkey says it's too weak, and suggests Bhima just lift its tail aside.

He can't. Not with one hand, not with both, not with everything he has.

The monkey is Hanuman, and he arranged the whole thing on purpose — to humble his younger half-brother before pride carried him into danger. But look at *how* he does it: no audience, no insult, no blow. He lets Bhima discover his own limit, explains the bigger picture, then embraces him and promises to protect his family in the war ahead.

That's the difference between correcting someone and crushing them. The people who most need to be humbled are rarely helped by humiliation. They're helped by meeting the edge of their own strength in private — and then being offered something larger to reach for.

The one who humbles you can become the one who has your back.
