## LinkedIn post
There's a moment in the Mahabharata that reads like a case study in the loneliness of leadership.

The half-rakshasa warrior Ghatotkacha is killed on the fourteenth night of the war. His father Bhima is inconsolable. The whole army grieves. And the commander — Krishna — celebrates.

Not from cruelty. Krishna was the only person tracking the weapon rather than the loss. Karna had held an infallible, single-use divine dart reserved for Arjuna. As long as he held it, the Pandavas' best warrior had no defence. Ghatotkacha's death forced Karna to spend it. The threat was gone.

The epic does something remarkable here: it lets the father's grief and the strategist's relief stand side by side without resolving them. Both are true. Anyone who has ever been accountable for an outcome that others could only mourn will recognise that tension.
