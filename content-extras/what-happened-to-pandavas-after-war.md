## LinkedIn post
We tend to treat winning as the end of the story. The Mahabharata disagrees.

The epic's most famous moment is the Pandavas' victory at Kurukshetra — but that victory lands well before the story's close. Nearly a third of the text covers what came after: a reluctant king ruling in guilt, an empire slowly coming apart, and five brothers who eventually renounce everything they fought for and walk out of the world on their own terms.

Read as a study in leadership and endings, the closing books are remarkably modern. Reaching the goal delivers responsibility, not rest. Everything acquired eventually has to be set down. And the flaws that undo people are rarely the obvious ones — the mightiest Pandavas fell over failings so small they'd gone unnoticed for a lifetime, while steadiness alone carried Yudhishthira to the end.
