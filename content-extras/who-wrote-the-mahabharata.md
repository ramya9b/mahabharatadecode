## LinkedIn post
"Who wrote the Mahabharata?" looks like a trivia question. It's actually a case study in how we assign authorship to anything large.

The tradition credits one sage, Vyasa. Modern scholarship describes an oral epic that grew over roughly eight centuries in many hands, later reconstructed manuscript-by-manuscript in the BORI Critical Edition (1919–1966). Both answers are true — because they answer different questions: *who is this work credited to?* versus *how did this text actually come to exist?*

That distinction is useful far beyond Sanskrit literature. Ask it of any codebase, research field, or long-running project and the fog clears: a guiding author-figure and a long chain of contributors usually coexist. Naming one doesn't erase the other.
