## LinkedIn post
There's a scene in the Mahabharata that reads like a leadership parable.

Arjuna — already the finest archer of his generation — goes into the mountains to earn the highest weapon on offer. He does everything right: fierce discipline, total focus, years of effort. Then a hunter picks a fight with him, and Arjuna loses. Completely. Arrows fail, bow taken, sword broken, pinned in a wrestling match.

The hunter turns out to be Shiva, testing him. And here's the part worth sitting with: the god wasn't testing whether Arjuna could shoot. He already knew that. He was testing whether Arjuna would keep going when he couldn't win — and then whether he'd have the humility to kneel when he finally understood who he was up against.

Only then did Shiva hand over the Pashupata, with a warning: never use it on a weaker opponent, or it burns the world.

The capability was never the qualification. The restraint was.
