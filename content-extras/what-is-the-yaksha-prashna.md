## LinkedIn post
There's a scene in the Mahabharata that reads like a leadership case study disguised as a myth.

Five brothers, exhausted near the end of a long exile, come to a lake. A guardian sets one rule: answer my questions before you drink. The four strongest, smartest, fastest of them ignore it and die on the spot. Only the eldest, Yudhishthira, stops to listen — and passes.

Two moments stand out for anyone who makes decisions under pressure:

1. Speed and strength were not the skills being tested. The willingness to pause and engage was. Four heroes rushed past the question and lost everything.

2. Offered the life of one dead brother, Yudhishthira chose the *least* strategically useful one — to keep faith with a promise of fairness no one was enforcing. When self-interest and fairness split, and you could get away with self-interest, what you do reveals what you actually value.

The episode is called the Yaksha Prashna. I wrote up the full story and the lessons it still teaches about slowing down, choosing fairly, and keeping death in view.
