## LinkedIn Post
The Mahabharata has a quiet lesson buried in the story of Pandu, father of the five Pandavas.

Pandu was a conquering king and a master archer. One careless act on a hunt — killing a sage who had taken the form of a deer — brought a curse: he would die the instant he ever embraced a wife again. It cost him his throne, his hope of children, and eventually his life.

What struck me rereading it is his response. He didn't deny the mistake or grow bitter. He gave up the crown, went to live simply, and tried to hold the line. He held for years. Then, one ordinary spring morning, being human caught up with him.

Two things worth carrying from it:
• Real accountability sometimes means surrendering what you value, not just saying sorry.
• Being warned about a danger is not the same as being safe from it.
