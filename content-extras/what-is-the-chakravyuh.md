## LinkedIn post
"Chakravyuh" has become business shorthand for a trap you can enter but can't escape — a debt chakravyuh, a bureaucratic chakravyuh. It's worth knowing what the original actually taught.

In the Mahabharata, the Chakravyuh was a battle formation: troops arranged in rotating concentric rings that let an attacker in and then closed behind him. On the war's thirteenth day, only a few warriors alive knew how to break it — and the two best were lured away by design. That left a sixteen-year-old, Abhimanyu, who knew one specific thing: how to get IN. He had never learned how to get OUT.

The strategic lesson is uncomfortably modern:

• The ability to *start* something ambitious is a real skill — and a completely different one from the ability to *finish or exit* it.
• The plan depended on allies following him through the breach. One determined blocker at the entrance turned "fighting alongside his family" into "dying alone." A plan that assumes backup arrives on time has a hidden single point of failure at exactly that handoff.
• Courage got him in. Courage alone could not get him out.
