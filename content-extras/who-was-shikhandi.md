## LinkedIn Post
The Mahabharata has a lesson buried in one of its most misunderstood characters: an injustice you refuse to address doesn't disappear. It waits.

Shikhandi is usually reduced to a battlefield tactic — the person Bhishma wouldn't fight, the shield Arjuna hid behind. But the epic spends real time on this figure, and it does something remarkable: it lets Bhishma himself narrate the whole backstory.

That backstory is Amba's — a woman wronged in a way that was technically within the rules of her world, and who found no institution willing to make it right. Kings turned her away. A warrior-sage couldn't help. So the wrong reorganised itself across a lifetime and returned as Shikhandi, at the one moment it could not be sent away again.

There's something here for anyone who has ever been told to "move on" from a wrong that followed procedure but left no real recourse. "Technically allowed" was never the end of the moral question.
