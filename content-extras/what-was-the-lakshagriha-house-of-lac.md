## LinkedIn post
One of the oldest lessons in the Mahabharata is about a very modern danger: the threat that arrives disguised as a gift.

In the episode of the Lakshagriha — the "house of lac" — Duryodhana tries to murder the five Pandavas not with an army but with hospitality. He has a splendid mansion built for them in a festival town, made entirely of flammable materials, meaning to burn them alive and call it an accident.

The Pandavas survive for three reasons worth thinking about:
• Someone honest (Vidura) warned them — at real personal risk, and in code so it couldn't be traced.
• They didn't panic or flee. They played along for months while quietly preparing an exit.
• They had redundancy: the warning AND a hidden tunnel, not one or the other.

There's also a hard edge the epic refuses to sand down — their escape cost seven innocent lives, and the text doesn't pretend otherwise.
