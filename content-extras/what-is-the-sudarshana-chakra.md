There's a leadership lesson hiding inside one of Hinduism's most famous weapons, and it isn't the one you'd expect.

The Sudarshana Chakra — Krishna's spinning discus — is imagined as constant, overwhelming force. But read the Mahabharata closely and something quieter emerges. Krishna spends the great war unarmed, as a charioteer. The discus is drawn to kill just once, and only after he has absorbed a hundred insults he had vowed to forgive.

Even the name works against the myth: "Sudarshana" means "fair to behold" or "good vision" — not "destroyer."

The weapon's authority comes from what everyone knows it could do, not from how often it's used. Power held in reserve, released only when a clearly defined line is crossed. That's a discipline worth borrowing.
