## LinkedIn post
There's an old Sanskrit verse, recited every morning for centuries, that names eight beings said to be deathless — the Chiranjivi, the "long-lived."

Read it as mythology and it's a curiosity. Read it as a leadership and life lesson and it's sharper than most modern advice.

Line the eight up and a pattern appears. The ones for whom immortality is a joy — Hanuman, Vyasa, Markandeya — all have something outside themselves to serve: devotion, knowledge, a refusal to let go of what matters. The one for whom it's agony — Ashwatthama — was left with nothing but himself and a wound that never heals.

The tradition isn't really claiming eight people never died. It's making a point most of us learn the hard way: duration without direction is not a prize. Time only becomes valuable when it's pointed at something beyond yourself.
