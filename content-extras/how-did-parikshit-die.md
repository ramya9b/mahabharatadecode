## LinkedIn Post
A leadership lesson buried in the Mahabharata, and it isn't about the war.

King Parikshit was, by every account, a good ruler — just, capable, decades of steady governance. Then, exhausted and hungry on a hunt, he had one bad moment. He insulted a meditating sage over nothing. That single lapse ended his life.

Three things in the story are worth sitting with:

1. A lifetime of good conduct did not cancel one careless act. The version of us that shows up on our worst afternoon still counts.

2. The greatest harm didn't come from a villain. It came from the sage's son, who wielded enormous power in a fit of secondhand rage — before he had checked what actually happened. His own father was horrified.

3. Parikshit prepared hard against the danger he could picture (a snake at the gate) and was undone by the one he couldn't (a serpent hidden in a fruit). Guarding the expected risk is not the same as being safe.

The catastrophe was built from ordinary overreactions that no one paused to stop. That is how most disasters actually get made.
