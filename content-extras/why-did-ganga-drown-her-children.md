## LinkedIn post
One of the oldest stories in the Mahabharata is really a lesson in judging actions from the outside.

A goddess marries a king on one condition: he must never question anything she does. She then drowns their first seven newborn sons in the river. He keeps silent, bound by his word, until the eighth — when he finally breaks and demands to know why.

The reveal reframes everything. The children were cursed celestial beings who had asked to be released from mortal life as quickly as possible. What looked like murder from the king's side of the river was, from theirs, mercy they had requested.

Two things I keep coming back to:
1. The same act can be cruelty or kindness depending on what you know. Context doesn't soften a deed — it can reverse its meaning entirely.
2. Some promises don't ask you to act. They ask you to stand still while something hard happens.

The eighth child, the one who couldn't be freed, grew up to become Bhishma — the epic's most steadfast and self-denying figure.
