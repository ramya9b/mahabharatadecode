## LinkedIn post
There's a leadership lesson hiding in one of the Mahabharata's most famous deaths.

On the seventeenth day of the Kurukshetra war, Karna — arguably the finest archer on the field — is winning his final duel when the earth swallows his chariot wheel. He reaches for his most powerful weapon and cannot remember how to summon it. Moments later, he's gone.

Two things undo him, and both are worth sitting with:

1. A curse that left all his skill intact and removed only his ability to access it at the decisive moment. That's not a story about mythology. That's a story about choking under pressure — the knowledge is present and momentarily unreachable. You don't fix it by learning more; you fix it by rehearsing recall under real pressure.

2. He appeals to the warrior's code of fairness at the exact instant he needs it — and is reminded of every time he broke that same code himself. A standard you've already broken loses its force in your mouth.
