## LinkedIn post
There's a story tucked inside the Mahabharata that I think about often, because it's really a story about negotiation and clarity under pressure.

Savitri is told, a year in advance, the exact date her husband Satyavan will die. She marries him anyway. When death arrives in person, she doesn't beg. She follows the god of death and engages him — calmly, on the subject of duty — until he offers her boons.

Then she does something remarkable. Three of her four requests are for other people. Only after she has proven she isn't asking selfishly does she ask for a hundred children by her husband. Yama grants it without thinking — and traps himself, because a dead man can't father children. To keep his word, he has to give Satyavan back.

She doesn't defeat death by force. She holds it to its own commitment.
