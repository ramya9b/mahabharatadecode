## LinkedIn post
The Mahabharata could have given Krishna any death it wanted. It chose the smallest one imaginable.

No final battle. No worthy enemy. Thirty-six years after the war, his clan self-destructs in a drunken brawl, his brother departs in meditation, and Krishna is killed by a hunter's stray arrow while resting in a forest, mistaken for a deer.

There's a leadership lesson buried in that ending, and it's an uncomfortable one. The epic spends eighteen books insisting that consequences reach everyone, and then refuses to let even its most capable character stand outside that law. The man who steered the outcome of the war does not get to skip the reckoning that follows it.

What stays with me is the last act: with the arrow in his foot and the hunter shaking in terror, Krishna's response is to forgive him. When an outcome is fixed, the manner in which you meet it is still entirely yours.
