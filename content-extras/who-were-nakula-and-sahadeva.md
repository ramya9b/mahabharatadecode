We can all name Yudhishthira, Bhima, and Arjuna. But the Pandavas were five — and the two youngest, the twins Nakula and Sahadeva, get maybe a line each in most retellings.

That's a shame, because their story is quietly one of the most human in the epic. Their mother Madri died on Pandu's funeral pyre when they were small, so they were raised by Kunti, their father's other wife. One grew into the handsomest man of his age and a master of horses. The other became so wise that later tradition says he could see the future but was forbidden to speak it.

And when Yudhishthira was given the chance to bring one dead brother back to life, he chose Nakula — not for love, but for fairness. Here's the full story of the brothers everyone forgets.

## LinkedIn post
The Mahabharata has a lesson about talent that most organisations still haven't learned.

Nakula and Sahadeva were two of the five Pandava brothers — as royal, as capable, and as loyal as the other three. Nakula was a master horseman and swordsman; Sahadeva was renowned for his judgment and learning. Sahadeva personally killed Shakuni, the strategist behind the entire war.

Yet in almost every retelling, the twins are a footnote. They didn't self-promote. They didn't have the loudest arc. And so the story of the war became the story of the three brothers who did.

There's something worth sitting with there: how easily real competence gets overlooked when it doesn't announce itself, and how a good leader (Yudhishthira, in the famous Yaksha Prashna episode) makes a point of counting the quiet people in.
