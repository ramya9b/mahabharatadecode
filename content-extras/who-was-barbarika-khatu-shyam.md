### LinkedIn post
There's a leadership lesson buried in one of India's most beloved devotional stories, and it's about restraint.

In the tradition, Barbarika was a warrior with three infallible arrows — enough to end the Mahabharata war single-handedly in minutes. He had vowed to always fight for the weaker side. It sounds noble. Krishna saw the flaw: because Barbarika could not lose, any side he joined would become the stronger one, forcing him to switch endlessly until both armies were destroyed. A good intention, attached to unchecked power and applied without tracking its consequences, becomes a machine for harm.

So Krishna asked for his head before the war — and Barbarika gave it willingly. He is remembered not for arrows he fired, but for arrows he never fired.

Two things I keep coming back to:
• Real strength often shows up as restraint — the capability you choose to hold back.
• A principle held rigidly, without checking where it actually leads, can quietly reverse on you.

We also drew a clear line between what's in the Mahabharata's critical text and what's later tradition. Barbarika's story sits mostly outside the epic, in the devotional tradition of Khatu Shyam — and that distinction is worth making honestly.
